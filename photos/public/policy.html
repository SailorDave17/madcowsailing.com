Photos

Who sees these photos

Anyone can look at a photo here once it is approved. Only people with an account the site's admins approved for the team, people with the team's invite link, and the team's coaches, who sign in, can send one, and nothing shows until one of the site's admins has checked it. To take a photo down, press "Remove this photo" under it, or email dave@madcowsailing.com.

Who can see a photo

Anyone who visits photos.madcowsailing.com, with no sign-in. They see every approved photo and its caption, and they can download a full-size copy.

Every page and every photo asks search engines not to list it. A search engine that follows the request leaves the photos out of its results, but it is a request, not a lock.

A photo waiting to be checked is never shown, and neither is one that was turned down.

Who can send a photo

Someone with an account one of the site's admins approved for a team. They sign in with their email address and password, and can then send photos to that team's albums only, from any phone or computer signed in to the account, for as long as an admin leaves the team on the account. A phone signed in to an account sends from the account, even if it has also opened the invite link.

Someone holding the team's invite link, which is for the team's parents, sailors and coaches. Opening the link on a phone lets that phone send photos for 90 days, with no account, and no name or email address. When the link is changed, every phone that opened the old one stops sending until it opens the new one.

The team's coaches can also send without the link. A coach signs in with their email address, and only an address the site's admins have put on the coaches' list gets in. Their phone can then send for 90 days, until the coach is taken off the list. Changing the invite link does not stop it. A coach's photos are checked like everyone else's.

Every photo is checked first

Nothing sent shows up straight away. One of the site's admins looks at each photo and its caption, and checks it against the media release that covers its team. The admin can edit or clear a caption before the photo goes up.

For Hoover JRT, that is the media release families sign when they register with the team. The admin turns down any photo they recognize as showing a sailor whose family opted out.

For COHSSA, it is COHSSA's season-registration release, which COHSSA issues as part of registering for its season. Each family chooses on it whether to give permission, and can take it back at any time. The admin turns down any photo they recognize as showing a sailor whose family said no, or took it back. On photos published online, the release says:

2. Post and publish those images and recordings, along with my sailor's full name, school, and team results, on:

  • COHSSA and member team websites
  • Social media accounts run by COHSSA or its member teams (e.g., Instagram, Facebook)
  • Fundraising and promotional materials, including sponsor presentations, donor appeals, recruiting flyers, and event programs

A check can miss one. If a photo is up that should not be, press "Remove this photo" under it, or email the address above, and it comes down.

A photo that is turned down is deleted for good.

A caption shows beside its photo once the photo is approved, so leave children's full names out of it.

Location and camera details stay on the phone

The page you send from makes three smaller copies of each photo on your phone, the largest at most 2,560 pixels on its long side, and sends only those. The copies carry no location, no camera make or model, and none of the original's other hidden details. When a photo arrives, the site strips any such details again, in case a copy carried some. Your original stays on your phone as it was.

What the site keeps

For each photo sent:

  • three copies of it, sized for the album page, the screen and download;
  • its caption, if it has one;
  • the album it was sent to;
  • when it was taken, and when it was sent;
  • which invite link it was sent with, and when that phone opened it, or for a coach's photo, only that a coach sent it;
  • for a photo sent from an account, which account sent it, and whether the account is a coach's.

A photo sent with the invite link, or by a coach, keeps nothing that names who sent it: no name, no account and no email address, and for a coach's photo, not which coach. Someone sending with the invite link gives none of those at all. The phone keeps one small cookie, which is what lets it send for 90 days. A coach's cookie holds their address only in a scrambled form, made with a secret key.

A photo sent from an account is different: the site records which account sent it, and only the site's admins see it.

Photos shared to the installed app from a phone's gallery wait in this site's storage on that phone, exactly as they were shared, location included, until each is sent or removed. Only the copies made for sending ever leave the phone. One not sent within a day is no longer offered, and is deleted the next time the app opens or is shared to.

The coaches' list itself holds each coach's email address, and only the site's owner can change it, in Cloudflare's dashboard. Cloudflare, which signs a coach in, records each sign-in in the site's account, with the coach's email address and network address, as it does for the admins. So when a coach's photo was sent could still be matched against who signed in shortly before.

The site also counts how many photos are sent in a day, so no more than 500 come from one account, or from one phone sending with the invite link or a coach's sign-in. An account's count is kept under the account, for every phone and computer signed in to it together. A phone's is kept under the invite link, or a coach's scrambled address, and the time the phone opened it. Each count is cleared the next day anyone sends a photo.

An invite link carrying an old or wrong code is refused. So that nobody can try code after code, the site records failed attempts, up to a set number each hour. It keeps the network address each attempt came from only in a scrambled form, made with a secret key, so it can match a repeat attempt without storing the address. An attempt counts for an hour, and is deleted the next time anyone opens an invite link after that.

When someone takes a photo down with "Remove this photo", nothing is deleted yet. The site keeps the photo's three copies, when it was taken down, and the note left with it, if any, which can hold a name and which only the site's admins read. The time and the note stay with the photo if an admin puts it back, and go with it when an admin deletes it.

So that nobody can take the whole site down at once, one network can take down at most 10 photos an hour. The site counts them by the network address each came from, kept only in the same scrambled form. A takedown counts for an hour. After that it is deleted the next time anyone takes a photo down or one of the site's admins opens the admin pages. Pressing it on a photo that is not showing counts for nothing, and nothing is kept.

The site runs on Cloudflare, which carries every visit to it. This page covers what the site itself keeps.

What an account keeps

If you ask for an account, as a parent, a coach or anyone else, for Hoover JRT, COHSSA or both, it keeps:

  • your name and email address;
  • your role, the teams you asked for, and which of them an admin approved;
  • whether you are one of the site's admins, and whether you are its owner;
  • when you asked, which teams still wait for an admin's answer, and whether the admins have been emailed about your request;
  • the note you left with your request, if any;
  • your password, only as a hash, which can check a password typed in but cannot be turned back into it;
  • how many times in a row signing in to it has failed, until a sign-in succeeds;
  • a link to set your password, while it waits to be used, only as a hash, which can check the link but cannot be turned back into it;
  • if you are an admin, each code emailed to you to sign in, only as a hash made with a secret key, and when it was sent;
  • which photos you sent from it.

On the site, only its admins see any of it. Every admin sees every account, for both teams. No public page, photo or download shows who sent a photo.

When an admin approves your account for a team, the site emails you a link to set your password. It can be used once, for 7 days, and a newer link replaces it once the newer one's email is sent. Once its 7 days are up, it is deleted the next time an admin opens the list of accounts or any link is sent. Setting a password with a link uses up every link the account holds.

Signing in leaves one small cookie on that phone or computer, which keeps it signed in for 90 days. The cookie holds your account's number, a session number and when you signed in, signed with a secret key so it cannot be changed, and no password, name or email address. Signing out, setting a new password, being made one of the site's admins, or an admin revoking the account for any team ends every session the account has, on every phone and computer, the next time each is used.

An admin's sign-in takes one more step. Once the password is right, the site emails a 6-digit code to the account's address. The code works once, for 10 minutes and 5 tries, and only in the browser where the password was typed, which holds a small cookie naming that sign-in for those 10 minutes. The code is kept only as a hash, made with a secret key, and when it was sent is kept for a day, so that the site sends an admin at most 10 codes a day; after that day it is deleted, the next time a code is sent or an admin opens the admin home page. Setting a new password deletes an account's codes at once. The right code opens a second cookie, for the admin pages, which lasts 12 hours and holds what the first one holds, and nothing more.

So that nobody can guess a password, the site counts failed sign-ins: at most 10 an hour for one email address, whether or not it has an account, and 20 an hour from one network. It keeps each failed try for an hour, with the email address that was typed and the network address it came from, both only in a scrambled form, made with a secret key. After that hour it is deleted, the next time a sign-in fails or one of the site's admins opens the admin home page, and a sign-in that succeeds deletes its address's tries at once. The site takes at most 100 failed sign-ins an hour from everyone together, and after that nobody can sign in until the hour is up. After 100 failed sign-ins in a row, an account's password stops working until a new one is set.

If you forget your password, the form to reset it emails a link to set a new one, but only to an address with an account the site's admins approved. The link works once, for an hour, and is kept only as a hash, like the link an approval sends. The site sends one account at most one link every 15 minutes while an earlier one waits to be used, and at most 20 reset emails a day for everyone together. One network can ask at most 10 times an hour, counted by its network address in the same scrambled form, which is kept for an hour and deleted after that, the next time anyone asks or one of the site's admins opens the admin home page.

When you set a password, the site checks it against passwords seen in data breaches elsewhere, using Have I Been Pwned's Pwned Passwords service. It sends the service only the first 5 characters of the password's SHA-1 hash, never the password, and gets back every breached hash that starts with them, so the service never learns which password was checked.

The admins also keep a log of what they do with each account: who approved it or turned it down for each team, changed its role, sent it a link to set a password, made it an admin or removed it as one, revoked it for a team, hid every photo it sent, deleted it, or let its address ask again after a delete, and when. Each entry names the person whose account it was, by name and email address. The log has no set limit.

The request asks for no sailor's name, so leave sailors' names out of the note too.

An account, and a request for one, is kept until it is deleted. There is no set limit.

Two other services handle a request. Cloudflare Turnstile checks that the request form, and the form to reset a password, were filled in by a person, not a program. To do that, it sees your network address, what your browser reports about itself, details of how it connects, and which site the form is on, but not what you type into the form. Cloudflare says it collects these to tell people from programs, not to identify, profile or target anyone, and also uses them to improve Turnstile.

So that nobody can flood the site with requests, one network can send at most 10 requests an hour, and the site takes at most 100 an hour from everyone together. It counts them by the network address each came from, kept only in a scrambled form, made with a secret key. A request counts for an hour, and is deleted after that, the next time the site takes a request or one of the site's admins opens the admin home page. Until then, the scrambled address is kept with the time of the request, to the second. A request that made an account gave the account that same time, so for that hour the two could be matched.

Resend sends the site's email. When you ask for an account, the site emails its admins through Resend with your name, role and teams, and the emails sent to you, such as the link to set your password, go through Resend too. Resend sees each email's address and everything in it, and keeps each one for 30 days.

How long photos stay

There is no set limit. An approved photo stays up until someone takes it down. A photo taken down stays hidden until an admin puts it back or deletes it for good.

Having a photo taken down

Press "Remove this photo" under it on its album page. It is hidden from everyone at once, with no sign-in and nothing to wait for. You can leave a note for the site's admins, up to 500 characters. An admin then reviews it, and either puts it back or deletes it for good.

Or email dave@madcowsailing.com with the photo attached, or its link copied from the album page. A photo's number in its album can change as other photos are added, so a number alone may point at the wrong one.

Having an account deleted

Email dave@madcowsailing.com and ask. An admin writes to the address on the account to check the request came from you, and deletes the account once you confirm: your name, email address, role, teams, note and password hash all go.

The photos you sent stay, approved or still waiting, and are checked as usual, but they no longer record which account sent them, only whether it was a coach's. One that had been taken down keeps only the day it was taken down, not the time. To have them taken down as well, press "Remove this photo" under each, or say so in the same email.

Some things stay. The admins' log keeps its entries about your account, and they still name you. If an admin had revoked the account, the site keeps its email address in a scrambled form, made with a secret key, so that a new request from it is still held back, until an admin lets it ask again. The count of photos the account sent that day stays, under the account's number, until the next day anyone sends a photo. Emails already sent are not deleted with it: Resend keeps each for its 30 days, and the email telling the admins about your request stays in their mailboxes.

The site's database can be put back as it was at any moment in the last 30 days, so a deleted account stays in those restore points, which only the site's owner can use, for up to 30 days.
